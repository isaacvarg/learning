K and M

Today's win: you can tell K from M by ear at full 20 wpm, and you can send both.

Learn the sound, not the dots

Every character in Morse has its own rhythm. Fluent operators hear a character the way you hear a word: as a single shape, not as dots and dashes to count. Pierpont warns that learning from a printed chart produces the "famous plateau", usually somewhere around 7–10 wpm. You end up translating "dot, dash, dot…" in your head, and that step can't keep up with faster code (Pierpont, The Art and Skill of Radio-Telegraphy). So this course gives you every character as a sound, spelled out as di and dah, never as a dot-and-dash chart.

We use the Koch method. You hear characters at your target speed from day one, starting with just two. A new character arrives once you copy the ones you have with about 90% accuracy (Pierpont; LCWO uses the same order). Your target is 20 wpm, so that's the speed you'll hear today.

At 20 wpm, a dit lasts 60 ms. A dah is three dits long, and the gap between the parts of one character is one dit. Speed is measured with the word PARIS, which is exactly 50 dits long (morsecode.world, Morse Code Timing; ITU-R M.1677-1).

Meet the pair

K

dah-di-dah

Long, short, long. It rocks back and forth.

M

dah-dah

Two long beats, then it's over. Shorter than K.

Play each one five or six times. Say "dah-di-dah" and "dah-dah" out loud along with it.

Tell them apart

Press play (or Space), then answer. Answer with your first instinct; you have five seconds. Don't stop to count.

Now at speed: a dozen in a row

K and M, mixed. Go straight down the list and answer each one fast. A mistake is fine: the app brings that letter back sooner.

Once you press Done, the Listen · Koch letters so far drill opens up with these letters. It grows with every new letter, so use it for extra rounds on any day.

Send them

Hold Space or the pad to key, and let go between parts. Hold a dah about three times as long as a dit, and leave a short gap, about one dit, between the parts. When you stop, the widget decodes what you sent. It shows your speed and your timing, meaning how far your lengths were from a clean 1 : 3 rhythm.

Get the rhythm right first; your speed will come up as you practise. Press Hear it whenever you want the model. Say the character in your head as you key it.
